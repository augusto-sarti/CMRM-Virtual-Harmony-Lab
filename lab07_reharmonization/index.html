Reharmonization

Keep a melody fixed while changing the harmonic path beneath it. The melody becomes a constraint: every new chord must explain the same sounding note in a musically plausible way.

How much can the harmony change while the melodic identity remains recognizable?

A useful reharmonization does not simply choose a chord that contains the melody note. It also has to preserve convincing function, bass motion, voice leading, and phrase direction.

4. Build the reharmonization

Version C is not a random collection of substitute chords. It changes the route in three intelligible operations.

Tonicize IV
Change the second Cmaj7 to C7, turning I into V7/IV and directing the phrase toward F.
2
Arrive on IV
Replace the Dm7 region with Fmaj7. The melody A becomes the 3rd of Fmaj7 rather than the 5th of Dm7.
3
Borrow iv from the parallel minor
Use Fm6 before G7. The fixed melody F becomes the root of the borrowed chord and creates a darker predominant colour.

5. Follow five continuous voices, not chord stacks

The soprano is completely fixed. The reharmonization is realized as five independent melodic strands, chosen globally across the whole phrase. The chord symbols stay the same, but inversions are allowed so that every voice can move smoothly.

Important: when a slash-bass realization appears below, it is not a new harmony. It is the same chord placed in an inversion to preserve melodic continuity among the voices.

What changed?

  • the chords beneath the melody
  • the harmonic role assigned to several melody notes
  • the bass path and inner-voice trajectories
  • the amount of chromaticism
  • the local sense of tension and release

What remained invariant?

  • the eight-note soprano phrase
  • the phrase length and rhythmic positions
  • the broad departure-and-return trajectory
  • the final arrival on C
  • the listener’s ability to recognize the melodic object

From reharmonization to expectation

The same melody can support several plausible harmonic continuations, but they are not equally expected. That question—how predictable a continuation is from its context—is the starting point of the final Harmony Lab.
