Harmony Lab 02 — Chord–Scale Duality

Treat a scale not only as an ordered pitch collection but as a generator of a harmonic vocabulary. Then reverse the view: accumulate chord tones and watch the reference collection reappear.

What changes, and what remains invariant?

1. Choose the reference collection

Start with C major, then change only one scale degree by moving E to E♭ and observe how many seventh chords are affected.

2:1 subsampling in scale-degree space: after the full scale, retain one degree out of every two from the selected root. The sequence 1–3–5–7 is then heard as an arpeggio and finally as a simultaneous seventh chord.

What changed?

  • one pitch in the reference collection: E ↔ E♭
  • the identity or quality of several generated seventh chords
  • the harmonic colour available to the vocabulary

What remained invariant?

  • seven scale degrees
  • the tertian construction rule: 1–3–5–7 within the collection
  • the idea of a reference collection as a generator
  • some individual chords, despite the changed collection
