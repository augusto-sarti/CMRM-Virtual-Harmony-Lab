4. Substitution & Harmonic Expansion

Learn a small set of operations that can change the harmonic surface without destroying its larger function: substitute a chord within a functional family, tonicize a target, and expand a dominant into a ii–V.

What can change while the destination remains recognizable?

1. Functional families — in context

Select a family. The highlighted card is the active experiment. Instead of comparing isolated chord stacks, hear the original and the substitute as voice-led realizations inside a short phrase.

Current harmonic context
Choose a family and compare the two realizations in context.

2. One destination, three approaches

Keep Fmaj7 fixed as the destination. Change only the way it is approached.

Invariant: every path ends on the same Fmaj7. What changes is the strength, chromaticism and temporal extent of the approach.

3. Hear the operations separately

A
Diatonic motion
Cmaj7 → Fmaj7. No local dominant is inserted.
B
Tonicization
C7 → Fmaj7. Changing B to B♭ turns C into V7/IV and creates directed pull toward F.
C
ii–V expansion
Gm7 → C7 → Fmaj7. The local dominant C7 is preceded by its own ii chord.

4. Melodic lines inside the expansion

The expanded path is not just three chord symbols. With a reasonable voicing, it becomes four simultaneous melodic strands.

Listen especially for the common tones G and B♭ from Gm7 to C7, followed by their stepwise motion into A and C at Fmaj7.

What changed?

  • the local chord used to express a function
  • the amount of chromaticism
  • the number of harmonic events before the target
  • the melodic paths taken by individual voices

What remained invariant?

  • the broader functional role of a substitution within the chosen context
  • the destination Fmaj7 in the tonicization/expansion experiment
  • the sense of directed arrival
  • several common tones across adjacent sonorities

Why this matters for the next labs

These are construction rules, not isolated tricks. In the blues lab we will start with a simple harmonic framework and apply such rules progressively. Tritone substitution is deliberately postponed to Lab 5, where its preserved guide tones can be examined in detail.
